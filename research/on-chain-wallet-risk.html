On-chain analysis

On-Chain Wallet Risk Analysis: Signals, Context and Limitations

Public ledgers make every transfer observable and almost nothing self-explanatory. This note is about the gap between the two: what a transaction graph supports, and where confident readings of it go wrong.

Published 2026-09-14 · Methodology note · Trustsniffer Security Research

On-chain analysis starts from an unusual advantage. Unlike web investigation, where a site can lie about itself and hide its history, the ledger is complete, public and immutable. Every transfer that ever happened is there, in order, with amounts.

The difficulty is that the ledger records movement and almost never records meaning. Addresses have no names. Two transfers with identical shape can be a salary payment and a laundering hop. The analytical work is entirely in reconstructing context that the chain does not store.

1. What an address actually is

Before assessing an address, establish what kind of object it is. The same score means different things for each.

Address types and what a risky counterparty implies for each
TypeTypical signatureWhat one tainted inbound transfer implies
Personal walletFew counterparties, irregular timing, one custody patternA lot — a small counterparty set makes each edge meaningful
Exchange deposit addressReceives from one user, forwards to a hot wallet, then dormantSomething about the depositor, not the exchange
Exchange hot walletEnormous degree, constant flow, both directions, many tokensAlmost nothing — it receives from everyone by design
Contract (DEX, bridge, mixer)Code at the address; flow determined by callersNothing about the contract; possibly something about the caller
Service or payout walletMany small outbound transfers on a scheduleDepends on whether the service screens its own inflows

Failing to classify first is the most common structural error in this work. A "high risk" verdict on a major exchange's hot wallet is not a finding; it is a description of what exchanges are.

2. Direct exposure versus indirect exposure

These are different claims and should never share a number.

Direct exposure means the address transacted with a flagged address itself: one hop, no intermediaries. It is a factual statement about a specific transaction that can be cited by hash.

Indirect exposure means value reached the address through intermediaries. It is a statement about a path, and its strength decays with every hop for reasons that are structural rather than conventional: each additional hop multiplies the number of plausible explanations, and most real-world paths pass through at least one address that mixes funds from many sources.

Once value enters a high-degree node — an exchange hot wallet, a large DEX pool, a bridge — the output side is no longer traceable to the input side by graph structure alone. Anything claimed "through" such a node is an inference about the service's internal accounting, not an observation of the chain. Serious methodologies treat those nodes as terminal: the trace stops, and says so.

Practical rule

State hop distance and path composition with every exposure claim. "Two hops, both through personal-looking wallets, 0.9 of the value" and "two hops, second through a major exchange" are different findings that a single percentage would render identically.

3. Reading fund flows

Direction, timing and proportion carry most of the signal.

  • Direction. Receiving from a flagged address is something that can be done to you; anyone can send funds to any address, unsolicited. Sending to one is a choice. The two must not be weighted equally, and dusting — tiny unsolicited transfers, sometimes deliberately from tainted sources — is a known technique for contaminating an innocent address's profile.
  • Proportion. A tainted transfer worth 0.0001% of a wallet's lifetime volume is noise. One representing most of its balance is the wallet's defining event.
  • Timing. Rapid onward movement after receipt, particularly splitting into equal amounts across new addresses, is a recognisable pattern. So is the reverse: consolidation of many small inbounds into one outbound, which is what both a laundering funnel and an ordinary merchant settlement look like.
  • Longevity. An address created, used once and abandoned tells a different story from one used continuously for three years.

Every one of those patterns has a legitimate twin. Equal-value splits are also how payroll and airdrops work. Consolidation is also what an accountant does. The pattern raises the question; the counterparties and the context answer it.

4. Association is not proof

This deserves stating directly, because the entire field's credibility depends on it.

An address appearing in a transaction with a flagged address establishes exactly one fact: a transfer occurred. It does not establish that the owners know each other, that the receiving party consented, that either understood the provenance, or that anyone committed an offence. Ownership itself is an inference — addresses are not identities, custody can be shared, keys can be stolen, and a single person routinely controls many addresses while a single address can represent many people.

The defensible language reflects this. "Received 4,200 USDT in three transfers from an address designated by OFAC on 12 March" is a citable fact. "Is linked to sanctioned entities" is a characterisation that the evidence does not support and that will not survive review.

Where on-chain false positives come from

Unsolicited inbound transfers and dusting; shared infrastructure such as exchange, bridge and pool addresses that receive from everyone; address reuse across unrelated purposes; stale attribution after an address changes hands or a designation is lifted; cross-chain identifier collisions; and naive multi-hop tracing that treats a high-degree node as transparent. These account for the large majority of wrong calls, and every one of them is detectable before the finding is written.

5. Issuer action is a fact; sanctions designation is a different fact

Stablecoin issuers can freeze balances at the contract level, and those freezes are observable on-chain as state changes. Sanctions designations are administrative decisions published by government bodies. They overlap but are not the same thing, and conflating them produces claims that are simply incorrect.

An issuer may freeze an address at law-enforcement request, after a hack, or under its own terms of service, with no sanctions designation involved. Conversely, a designated address may never be frozen on any particular token contract. When publishing or consuming a list, insist on knowing which of the two a row represents — our own frozen stablecoin wallet dataset keeps them in separate columns for exactly this reason, and the sanctions research note covers the designation side in depth.

6. An order of work that avoids the usual traps

  • Normalise and validate the address, and confirm the chain. A valid-looking string on the wrong network is a different address entirely.
  • Classify the address type before scoring anything: degree, lifetime, counterparty diversity, whether code is deployed there.
  • Separate inbound from outbound and record them separately.
  • Identify direct counterparties that carry an independent designation or attribution, and cite the transaction hashes.
  • Extend outward hop by hop, stopping at any node whose degree makes the path uninterpretable, and record where you stopped.
  • Express exposure in value proportion as well as in count. Three tainted transfers out of four matter; three out of forty thousand generally do not.
  • Check whether any designation you relied on is still current, and date the finding.

For a single address, a public crypto wallet risk checker will do the classification and first-hop exposure pass for you, which is a reasonable starting point before deciding whether a deeper manual trace is warranted.

7. What a good output looks like

A useful wallet assessment states the address and chain; the classification and the evidence for it; direct exposure with hashes and dates; indirect exposure with hop distance, path composition and the point at which tracing stopped; the value proportion; the designations relied on with their sources and dates; and an explicit statement of what was not determined.

That last element is the one most often missing and the one that most distinguishes research from assertion. "No evidence of direct exposure was found within four hops as of 14 September 2026" is a statement someone can act on. "Clean" is not.
