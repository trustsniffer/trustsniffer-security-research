Sanctioned & Frozen USDT and USDC Wallets

10,518 addresses whose most recent recorded state on the USDT or USDC contract is frozen — 3,130 on Ethereum and 7,388 on Tron. Government designations are kept in a separate column from issuer freezes, because they are different facts.

Snapshot 2026-09-14 · CSV + JSONL · CC BY 4.0

  • Tron — sanctioned-wallets-tron.csv · JSONL (7,388 addresses, USDT)

What is in it, precisely

Stablecoin issuers can freeze a balance at the token-contract level. That action is observable on-chain and is recorded here. Separately, government authorities designate addresses under sanctions programmes and seizure orders. The two overlap but are not the same fact, and a list that merges them will misdescribe most of its own rows.

Every row therefore carries a designation value:

Designation values and what each one asserts
ValueAssertsEthereumTron
sanctions_listedThe record carries a government designation or seizure-order attribution (for example OFAC, NBCTF, a national seizure order) in addition to the freeze.127812
issuer_freezeThe token issuer blacklisted the address on the contract. Sometimes at law-enforcement request, sometimes under its own terms. Not by itself a government sanction.3,0036,576

Across both chains, 939 of 10,518 rows carry a sanctions attribution. The remainder are issuer actions, and describing them as "sanctioned" would be wrong.

How it was built

The underlying feed is an append-only event log: a freeze writes one record, an unfreeze writes another. Publishing the log directly would republish addresses that have since been released, so the build takes the latest event per address per chain and keeps only those whose latest state is frozen. On this snapshot that excluded 384 Ethereum and 954 Tron addresses that had been unfrozen.

Addresses are then validated against each chain's format. 36 malformed Tron strings were excluded rather than repaired — guessing a missing character would be inventing data, and a malformed address in a screening list is worse than a missing one.

Scope is deliberately narrow: USDT and USDC only, Ethereum and Tron only. Freezes on other tokens and other networks exist and are out of scope for this file.

Limitations — read before use

This is a point-in-time snapshot, not a live feed. Freezes are lifted and designations are amended; a row can be stale the day after publication. It is not a sanctions list and is not a substitute for one: the authoritative record for any designation is the publishing authority, and the authoritative record for any freeze is the token contract itself. A frozen address does not establish that its owner committed an offence — issuers freeze on suspicion, on request, and sometimes on addresses that merely received tainted funds.

Suggested use

  • As a research corpus for studying freeze patterns, timing and concentration across the two chains.
  • As a cross-reference when investigating a specific address, alongside a live check of the contract.
  • As a starting index for entity research — never as the final word on a designation.
  • Filter on designation before describing anything as sanctioned.

The methodology behind the distinction is set out in Sanctions Research for Digital Risk Investigations, and the reasoning about exposure and association in On-Chain Wallet Risk Analysis. For checking a single address interactively rather than in bulk, Trustsniffer's public wallet risk tool covers the same two chains.
