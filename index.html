Web & Blockchain Risk Intelligence Research

Notes on how digital risk is actually assessed: what each signal can tell you, what it cannot, and why a single indicator almost never settles a question about a website, a wallet or an entity.

What this site is

Risk analysis of internet infrastructure has an awkward property: nearly every individual signal is ambiguous. A domain registered last week might be a phishing kit or a startup that launched on Tuesday. An address two hops from a sanctioned wallet might be a launderer or an exchange that processed a deposit it had no way to refuse. Treated alone, each observation produces a confident answer that is wrong a meaningful share of the time.

These pages document the methodology side of that problem, in public, for people who do this work: fraud and compliance analysts, trust and safety teams, blockchain investigators, and engineers building detection systems. They are written by the team behind Trustsniffer, a platform that analyses website trust signals and on-chain exposure, and they describe the reasoning rather than a product.

Nothing here is a scoring formula to copy. The useful part of this kind of work is knowing which question each signal answers, how confidently, and what evidence has to accompany it before a conclusion is defensible.

The shape of the problem: one signal is a question, not an answer

It is worth stating the core idea once, plainly, because every page here is an application of it.

Suppose you observe that a website's TLS certificate was issued three days ago. On its own that fact supports at least four competing explanations: the site is new; the site rotated certificates on a 90-day schedule, which is the default for a large share of the web; the site migrated hosting; or the site is a short-lived phishing deployment. The observation is real and the inference is not determined. What changes the picture is the combination: a three-day-old certificate on a three-day-old domain, resolving to a host with no other legitimate tenants, serving a login form that posts to a different origin, is a different object entirely.

The same structure appears on-chain. A wallet that received funds from an address later added to a sanctions list has done something that is either meaningless (it is an exchange hot wallet that processes millions of deposits) or decisive (it is a personal wallet whose only counterparty is that address). Direction, timing, amount relative to the wallet's history and the nature of the counterparty all change the reading.

Working principle

Treat every individual indicator as evidence for a hypothesis, never as a verdict. Record what the indicator would look like if the benign explanation were true — and check whether it does.

How these notes are organised

Each research page follows the same arc: what is observable, what each observation supports, where it breaks, and what a defensible write-up contains. The multi-signal page is the one that ties the others together, and is a reasonable place to start if you only read one.

Where a concept is easier to understand against a live example, the pages link to the relevant public Trustsniffer surface. Those links exist because the tools are useful for following along, not because the argument depends on them.
